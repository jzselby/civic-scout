## Top stories

- **SLC School District board to vote on up to $180 million in bonds**: On Nov. 4 the Salt Lake City School District board takes up a resolution authorizing the issuance and sale of up to $180,000,000 in general obligation bonds. The board will also hear a construction update and a budget report. Its consent agenda includes policy revisions on student mental health, traffic safety and the SHiNE salary supplement program. ([Public meeting notices](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1119643.html))

- **Developer plans 325 market-rate apartments on vacant Snellgrove site in Sugar House**: Low Property Group plans 325 rental apartments, 20,000 sq ft of retail, a public atrium and about 510 parking spaces on the 3.25-acre former Snellgrove parcel on 2100 South near 800 East. The project is allowed by right at up to 75 feet, so it needs no council approval, and it includes no income-restricted units. Construction could start next summer. Neighbors raised concerns about rents, traffic access and the Snellgrove ice cream sign. ([Sugar House Community Council](https://sugarhousecouncil.org/articles/news/developer-proposes-325-apartments-retail-and-public-atrium-for-former-snellgrove-site/))

- **SLC Council backs owner-occupancy rule for small-lot homes in single-family zones; library bond would cost households about $57 a year**: In the Oct. 20 work session, all council members supported adding an owner-occupancy requirement to Expanding Housing Options. The proposal would allow new small-lot homes by right and loosen standards in R-1, R-2 and SR zones. Members backed the flag-lot recommendations 6-1, with Sarah Young opposed. Hearings are Nov. 10 and 17, with tentative action Dec. 1. Members generally supported the library's roughly $90 million capital plan, which includes an $85 million Local Building Authority bond that would cost about $57 a year per household. The e-bike and water conservation items were postponed. ([SLC Council actions](https://slc.primegov.com/Portal/Meeting?compiledMeetingDocumentFileId=45847))
  *Earlier:* Planning Commission weighed the citywide housing change ([Oct. 5](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1107885.html)); council briefed on library bonds and small-lot changes ([Oct. 16](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1115957.html)); small-lot hearing set ([Oct. 30](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1119205.html)). New today: the straw-poll results and the per-household bond cost.

- **Weber County asks Inland Port Authority to take in land next to Ogden-Hinckley Airport**: On Nov. 3, Weber County commissioners vote on a resolution asking the Utah Inland Port Authority to include unincorporated property next to Ogden-Hinckley Airport in a port project area. Commissioners also hold first readings on fee changes at the Golden Spike Event Center and on solid waste fees. ([Public meeting notices](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1119587.html))

## Coming up

- **Comment deadline Nov. 16: Heber Valley rail trail wetland fill.** The Utah Division of Water Quality intends to certify Wasatch County's 2.2-mile Heber Valley Railroad Trail Phase B. The project would permanently fill about 0.60 acres of wetlands and add bridges over Snake Creek and the Provo River. The county proposes a 4-acre wetland mitigation site, and construction is planned for March to October 2027. ([Public meeting notices](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1119633.html))
- **Nov. 3 meeting and Nov. 10 hearing: Salt Lake County Council.**
  - On Nov. 3 the council votes on a countywide EV charging station policy and a new fee at county-owned charging stations.
  - It also holds a 2027 budget workshop covering the assessor, emergency services, justice courts and the TRCC fund long-range plan.
  - Consent items include a $150,000 open-space land purchase from Don and Teresa Skaggs.
  - The council sets a Nov. 10 public hearing on possible 2027 raises for executive county officers.
  
  ([Public meeting notices](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/notice/1119311.html))
- **Nov. 4: Sugar House Community Council.** The agenda includes a state Department of Alcoholic Beverage Services construction update on the new Sugar House liquor store and an update on Fire Station #3. The council will also consider CIP applications, hear a talk by Dr. Brian Moench of Utah Physicians for a Healthy Environment, and get a report from council member Sarah Young. ([Sugar House Community Council](https://sugarhousecouncil.org/articles/agendas/sugar-house-community-council-november-4th-2026-meeting-agenda/))

## Also notable

- **Sugar House Zions Bank to be torn down and rebuilt under apartments:** The land-use committee's next meeting at Sprague Library will review GEM Sugar House, which would rebuild the bank on the ground floor of an apartment building. The committee report also says the Snellgrove project is now called SUGARGROVE, the Planning Commission denied a drive-thru in the Sugar House Business District, and the commission approved a design review process for Affordable Housing Incentives. ([Sugar House Community Council](https://sugarhousecouncil.org/articles/luz-reports/shcc-luz-report-2026-november/))
- **S-Line track crossing on Highland Drive to close in November:** At the Oct. 7 Sugar House council meeting, UTA and Kiewit said S-Line trains will run next summer, with a November track-crossing closure on Highland Drive. The Highland High rebuild is on schedule for fall 2029. The meeting's AI-drafted summary gives a summer 2025 start for the Snellgrove project, which conflicts with the council's later article saying next summer. ([Sugar House Community Council](https://sugarhousecouncil.org/articles/public-meeting-review/monthly-meeting-10-7-26/))
