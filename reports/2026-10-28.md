## Top stories

- **Every Salt Lake City Council member objects to fuel pipeline route through Emigration Canyon and city watershed.** Minutes of the Oct. 13 work session show every council member raised concerns about or opposition to HF Sinclair's proposed Westline Connect pipeline through Emigration Canyon and the city's watershed, and HF Sinclair declined to commit to a route outside Salt Lake City. The council then voted 7-0 to go into a closed session on "reasonably imminent litigation." Separately, the Ballpark rezoning follow-up added about five acres of open space. ([SLC Council actions](https://slc.primegov.com/Portal/Meeting?compiledMeetingDocumentFileId=45754))
- **Out-of-state money drives $2.3 million ballot campaign.** Utahns Protecting Our Constitution raised $2,301,481, including about $1.6 million from the D.C.-based Fairness Project, $200,000 from Unite America, $150,000 from the National Education Association and $100,000 from Chuck Maggelet. It spent $2,754,467, mostly on Viewpoint Media ads. ([Campaign finance reports](https://disclosures.utah.gov/Search/AdvancedSearch))
- **Draper's Frosty Group puts $400,000 into PAC funding Democrats.** Life Elevated raised $424,526, including $400,000 from The Frosty Group on Oct. 19. It spent $380,456, including $200,000 to the Utah Democratic Party, $25,000 to Michael Finch, $20,000 to Celeste Johnson, and $15,000 each to CJ for UT Senate, Fitisemanu, Liz Oates and Ashlee Matthews. ([Campaign finance reports](https://disclosures.utah.gov/Search/AdvancedSearch))
- **Business leaders seed new PAC with $325,000.** Beehive Coalition PAC reported $100,000 each from Casey Baugh, Jeremy Andrus and Scott Anderson and $25,000 from Crystal Maggelet. It had spent only $52 as of Oct. 22. ([Campaign finance reports](https://disclosures.utah.gov/Search/AdvancedSearch))
